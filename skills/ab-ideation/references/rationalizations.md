# Common rationalizations

Loaded on demand from SKILL.md when tempted to skip a phase, drop the rejection summary or make the user's choice for them.

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I already know what to build, skip ideation" | Then use the ab-brainstorming skill directly. Ideation is for "I don't know what's worth doing" — not for re-ranking a foregone conclusion. |
| "More ideas is better" | 25 shallow ideas lose to 8 strong ones more often than not. The critique pass matters more than the volume. |
| "Skip the rejection summary, only show survivors" | The rejected list is half the value — it tells the user what was considered and why it didn't survive. Without it, survivors look arbitrary. |
| "I'll skip the codebase scan and ideate from intuition" | Ungrounded ideas drift toward generic advice. The signal is in what *this* codebase needs, not what any codebase might. |
| "Just hand the top idea to the ab-brainstorming skill automatically" | The user picks. Ideation produces a ranked menu; planning operates on the chosen item. Automating the choice removes the user's leverage. |
| "The painkiller-vs-vitamin distinction (does it relieve real pain, or is it merely nice to have?) is too philosophical" | It's the cheapest filter we have. Vitamins look reasonable in a list; under the painkiller test, most evaporate. |

## Key principles

- **Ground before ideating**: scan the actual codebase first, because ungrounded ideas drift toward advice that fits any codebase.
- **Generate many, critique all, explain survivors**: quality comes from explicit rejection, not optimistic ranking.
- **Route to the ab-brainstorming skill**: ideation identifies directions; ab-brainstorming designs the selected one, and ab-writing-plans then plans it.
